## Outcome and scope

The AI must search legal target, rummage, sacrifice and transformation choices as decisions within its turn sequence, rather than having policy preselect them before search. It must compare the payoff of an attack/sacrifice followed by retrieval/replay against keeping the creature alive. Slime Launcher is a regression example, not a production special case. Strength remains bounded and approximate; do not promise perfect play or a human win-rate target.

The user clarified the rank mapping after implementation: Orbital 3, Lunar 4, Planetary 5, Solar 6, Nebular 7, Galactic 8, Cosmic 9, and every Legend tier 10. All three sub-ranks within a named tier share its difficulty. For brackets 0..20, level = 3 + floor(clamp(bracket, 0, 20) / 3). This supersedes the earlier interpolation that reached 10 at Cosmic I. Preserve manual Easy/Medium/Hard levels 1/5/10 and all ladder awards/rank records. Increment the AI policy version so new stats distinguish this policy. Do not rewrite historical games or change card/rules versions merely for AI changes.

No card balance, rule changes, account/schema/API changes, new dependencies, learned models, network services, deck-builder rewrite or replacement website. Preserve existing opponent rollout information handling; do not introduce new access to hidden information. Human skill validation remains playtesting, not something a small AI-vs-AI sample can prove.

## Grounding and approach

- scripts/game/mcts_ai.gd:116-173 searches atomic actions with 140 iterations at level 10 and 24 top-up iterations; :295 predicts later turns using greedy completion. The search already clones actual rules states, validates commands and yields periodically. Extend this owner instead of adding card-specific plans or another rules implementation.
- scripts/game/headless_turn.gd:26-52 preselects a single stratagem target; :110 automatically resolves every resulting choice; :244-410 has policy readiness/target restrictions. This excludes legal alternatives before search. :419-459 resolves pending choices through one heuristic answer.
- scripts/game/ai_heuristics.gd:229 fixes damage targeting to an enemy creature if any exists, :272 chooses the highest-cost rummage option, and :293 scores hero health, board stats, hand count and mana. Runtime effects can reveal combos in rollouts, but this score does not explicitly value relevant reusable resources or engine abilities.
- scripts/game/game_state.gd:343-505 owns pending choice options, decline permissions and multi-step choice lifecycle; :937 validates commands and :1096 executes them. Keep it authoritative. Answer commands contain actor, choice_id and selection. Card/minion identity remains uid/instance_id; no live object references in actions.
- scripts/game/game_manager.gd:141 answers live AI choices with the fixed heuristic; scripts/game/ai_controller.gd:36 invokes that driver after each real action. Both must use the same searched choices as simulation, including reactive AI choices on a human turn. Human choices must still prompt the human.
- scripts/game/ranked_progress.gd owns rank difficulty. server/test/fixtures/ai_controls.gd currently asserts level 10 everywhere and must change with the intended curve. Website ranked guidance and .docs/ai.md must describe the restored behavior accurately.

Keep existing MCTS/clone owners and reusable tree flow. Expose pending choices as atomic answer actions: enumerate the rules-provided options plus decline only when allowed, stop search application at an owned choice, and branch without answering it through the old heuristic. Enumerate meaningful legal stratagem targets using existing targeting semantics/validation; do not retain policy-only exclusions such as refusing healthy bounce targets. Keep no-target effects to one action rather than many equivalent target variants. Greedy fallback/simulation remains usable for callers that expect a completed action, and may rank choices by simulated outcomes without discarding alternatives from search.

The live choice driver should accept/reuse the acting search context where possible, execute answers through the same core and advance/invalidate the tree at each real decision. Revalidate state after awaits; never prompt/act for the wrong owner, reuse a stale choice, or silently cancel a valid human decision. Reactive opponent choices in simulations use an opponent policy, not cooperative maximization for the searching side. Protect reuse with a fingerprint covering actual pending choice content and relevant hand/graveyard/engine state, not queue lengths alone.

Improve evaluation at shared AI owners around generic mechanics: actionable damage/readiness, persistent engines, retrieval availability and affordable replay potential. A graveyard is not free hand advantage: discount or omit its value when no retrieval/payoff exists. Do not encode card IDs, fixed faction scripts, or automatically sacrifice a creature merely because it can be retrieved. Keep terminal win/loss dominant. If needed, use bounded search guidance or retention of a proven winning line so adding choice branches does not make tactical play less reliable; internal search ordering/budgets may be calibrated from proof rather than fixed by this document.
